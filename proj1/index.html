Project 1

Images of the Russian Empire

Colorizing the Prokudin-Gorskii photo collection by aligning three glass-plate exposures into a single color photograph.

OverviewThe Problem

Sergei Prokudin-Gorskii photographed the Russian Empire with three exposures, each through a different color filter. In order to align this, the three channels must be translated relative to each other to minimize the small differences in camera position between them. The goal of this project is to implement a simple alignment algorithm and apply it to a set of example images.

MethodHow Alignment Works

Similarity metric. Channels are compared with the L2 distance (sum of squared differences) after each is normalized to zero mean and unit variance. On normalized images this is essentially equivalent to Normalized Cross-Correlation (NCC): minimizing the L2 distance maximizes the dot product of the two normalized images.

Border cropping. The scanned plates carry thick black and white borders that differ between channels. Because the L2 distance squares every difference, those saturated edges dominate the score and drag the optimum away from the true alignment. Scoring is therefore restricted to the central region, trimming the border from each image

Per-image normalization. Each channel is standardized to zero mean and unit variance before comparison. Without this the score is dominated by a constant brightness offset between plates that carries no alignment information at all. For example, in church.tif, the blue region has a much higher brightness, leading to it dominating the score.

Multi-scale pyramid. Full-resolution glass plates are ~3600 px on a side, with displacements up to ~180 px. An exhaustive search at that scale would be computationally expensive, so an image pyramid is built by repeatedly halving the image until it reaches a minimum size. The lowest level searches a wide [-40, 40] window, and we slowly upscale and refine the image by using the displacement found below it to lower the search space. Because each level is exactly a factor of two, doubling the previous estimate is a near-perfect prediction, so a handful of candidates suffices.

Part 1Single-Scale Alignment — Low-Resolution JPEGs

Exhaustive search over a [-15, 15] window at full resolution, minimizing the normalized L2 distance (essentially NCC). Displacements are given as (x, y), positive x right and positive y down, relative to the blue channel.

Part 2Multi-Scale Pyramid — Full-Resolution Glass Plates

All fourteen provided example images, aligned at full .tif resolution using the image pyramid described above. The three low-resolution JPEGs are included again here for completeness.

Compared to single-scale search, the pyramid aligns a full-size .tif about 200× faster (~15 s vs. ~52 min on church.tif).

Part 3Additional Plates from the Collection

Three further glass plates downloaded directly from the Library of Congress Prokudin-Gorskii collection and run through the same pipeline with no changes or per-image tuning. Each is a full three-frame negative of roughly the same size as the course images (~3,800 × 9,700 px).
